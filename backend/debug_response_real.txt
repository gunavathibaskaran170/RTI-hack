Here is a structured overview of the legal landscape surrounding your situation. Please note that property and criminal procedure laws vary significantly by jurisdiction, so this information is general and should be verified with a licensed attorney in your area.

### 🔍 1. Civil Boundary Dispute vs. Criminal Trespass
- **Civil Matter:** Boundary wall disputes are typically resolved in civil court through claims for declaration of title, injunction, partition, or specific performance. The focus is on property rights, survey lines, deeds, and historical possession.
- **Criminal Trespass:** Generally requires proof of **unlawful entry or occupation** coupled with **criminal intent** (e.g., knowingly entering or remaining on another’s property without permission, often with intent to intimidate, damage, or deprive possession). Mere disagreement over a boundary line or construction of a wall near a disputed line rarely meets the threshold for criminal trespass.

### ⚖️ 2. Impact of Your Pending Civil Suit (Since 2024)
- **Parallel Proceedings Risk:** Filing a criminal trespass complaint while a civil suit on the same property is pending can raise procedural and strategic issues. Many courts view this as an attempt to circumvent civil procedure or exert undue pressure.
- **Judicial Response:** Criminal courts often dismiss trespass complaints that are essentially civil boundary disputes, especially when a civil case is already active. Some jurisdictions may stay criminal proceedings pending civil resolution, or your neighbor could file a counter-complaint for abuse of process or malicious prosecution.
- **Res Judicata / Issue Estoppel:** If the civil court eventually decides the boundary, that determination may preclude relitigating the same facts in a criminal forum.

### 📜 3. How to Obtain Land Registration Details
Land records are generally public. Access methods vary by jurisdiction but commonly include:
- **Local Land Registry / Sub-Registrar Office:** Request title deeds, mutation records, survey maps, and ownership history.
- **County/City Recorder or Assessor’s Office:** Provides parcel maps, deed transfers, and tax records.
- **Online Portals:** Many jurisdictions offer searchable databases (e.g., GIS mapping, e-registry, land records portals).
- **Required Information:** Property address, survey/plot number, owner’s name, or deed reference. Some offices require a notarized request or fee.
- **Legal Use:** These records can support your civil case (e.g., proving title, historical boundaries, or encroachments) but do not automatically establish criminal trespass.
